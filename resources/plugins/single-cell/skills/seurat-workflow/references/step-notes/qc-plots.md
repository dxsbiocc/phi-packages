Purpose: see the distributions the thresholds will cut. Principle: the violin
shape shows the empty-droplet shoulder and the doublet tail; the joint scatters
catch what one-dimensional plots hide (many genes AND high mitochondrial
fraction = stressed, not healthy-and-busy). Interpretation: with several
samples, plot per sample — one pooled violin hides a bad library. Pitfalls:
choose thresholds from these plots plus the quantiles, then re-read the
after-plots; a threshold that deletes a whole shoulder of real cells is a
decision to revisit, not a result.
